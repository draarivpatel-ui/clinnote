Discharge notes, explained term by term.

ClinNote rewrites dense hospital discharge shorthand (PO BID q12h PRN, eGFR, SpO2) into plain English or Spanish, links every explained term back to the exact characters in the original note, lays the doses out on a 24-hour clock, and checks common over-the-counter add-ons for collisions. Its four preset notes score between Grade 11.5 and 16.2 on the Flesch-Kincaid formula.

    Safety, limits and who is affected

    Who benefits

    • Patients and family caregivers who go home with a discharge note full of shorthand and need to know what each line asks them to do.
    • Spanish-speaking households, for the four preset notes, which have curated Spanish text and a bilingual printable schedule.
    • Nurses and pharmacists who want a quick term-by-term check of which phrases in a note still need explaining, with the exact source span shown.

    Who could be harmed, and how

    • A reader who trusts the plain-language text over the clinician. The engine misses terms outside its 38-entry lexicon (held-out recall pending), and a missed term stays unexplained.
    • A reader of a note with negation. Negated terms (such as "No dyspnea") are struck through in the source spans and flagged in the audit trail, but the term is still explained; clinical meaning must be confirmed by a clinician.
    • A reader who treats a verified claim as clinical truth. The claim check confirms that a dose, lab value or date appears near its subject in the source; it cannot tell whether the advice itself is right, and claims it cannot extract pass through unchecked.
    • Spanish readers of pasted custom notes. Custom Spanish output is word substitution, not translation, and needs an interpreter.
    • Anyone relying on the add-on check for an item not on its list of four. An unlisted supplement shows no collision, which is not the same as safe.

    Limitations

    • Every number on this page comes from synthetic notes written by one author. No real patient records were used and no patient comprehension study was run.
    • Readability is the English Flesch-Kincaid formula. It measures sentence and word length, not understanding, and it is not valid for Spanish.
    • The triage panel is a fixed checklist with set outcomes. It does not diagnose, and in doubt the answer is always to call 911.
    • The dose clock has eight fixed slots, so real prescribed times can be rounded to the nearest slot.

    Privacy and clinical disclaimer

    100% Client-Side Privacy Architecture (Zero PHI Transmission): ClinNote executes its entire 38-term clinical NLP de-jargonizer, Flesch-Kincaid readability engine, 24-hour dosing timeline, and pharmacokinetic collision matrix locally inside your browser using pure JavaScript ES modules. A strict Content-Security-Policy with connect-src 'none' guarantees that zero patient health information (PHI) or custom discharge text is ever transmitted to any external server or cloud API.

    Educational Use & Clinical Disclaimer: ClinNote is an educational clinical communication workbench. Its rules, readability formulas, and benchmark suite are deterministic and unit tested, but the tool does not diagnose, prescribe, or replace direct physician, pharmacist, or interpreter consultation. The Evaluation drawer documents the conditions under which the engine fails.
